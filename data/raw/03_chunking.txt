CHUNKING IN RAG

Chunking divides a large document into smaller retrieval units.

Why chunk?
Sending an entire 100-page document for every question is expensive and often irrelevant. Retrieval works better when the system can select focused pieces.

Two important parameters are:
- chunk size
- chunk overlap

Chunk size determines approximately how much text is stored in each chunk.

Small chunks:
Pros: precise retrieval, less irrelevant context.
Cons: important relationships may be split.

Large chunks:
Pros: more context and complete ideas.
Cons: retrieval becomes less focused and uses more context.

Overlap repeats a portion of the previous chunk in the next chunk.

Example:
Chunk size = 500 tokens
Overlap = 100 tokens

Chunk 1: tokens 1-500
Chunk 2: tokens 401-900
Chunk 3: tokens 801-1300

The 100-token overlap helps preserve context around boundaries.

Do not choose chunk size only because a tutorial says "500 tokens."

Ask:
- What is the natural unit of meaning?
- Are headings important?
- Are tables important?
- Do paragraphs depend on each other?
- Does the source contain procedures?
- Are there references such as "as described above"?

Semantic boundaries are often better than arbitrary boundaries.

Examples:
FAQ -> one question/answer can be a chunk.
Technical documentation -> heading plus related paragraphs.
Code -> function/class/module.
Legal document -> clause or logically connected section.

A good chunk should ideally answer or substantially support a question by itself.

Chunk quality can be evaluated by asking:
1. Is the chunk understandable without its neighbors?
2. Does it preserve important entities and relationships?
3. Does it contain unnecessary unrelated content?
4. Would embedding this chunk represent the intended concept?
5. Can the retrieved chunk provide enough evidence for the LLM?

Chunking is an experiment, not a fixed constant.
