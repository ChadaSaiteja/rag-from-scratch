RAG Pipeline Playground

Every step of Retrieval-Augmented Generation on one page. Part A prepares the documents once. Part B runs for every question: from your sentence to the exact prompt an LLM would receive.

No AI model runs here. Vectors are TF-IDF keyword representations over this repo's 585-word vocabulary, computed in your browser with the same math as the other visualizations. Chunks, BM25, rank fusion and the prompt template are the real ones.

A · Indexing (once)B · Answering (per question)
Part A

Indexing: prepare the documents

  1. 1

    Load the document

    A raw lesson file is read from data/raw/. Pick any of the 10 lessons above.

  2. 2

    Clean the text

    Normalise whitespace and strip invisible characters so chunks and vectors aren't polluted by noise. This repo's ingest.py skips this step because the lessons are already clean.

  3. 3

    Split into chunks

    The text is cut into pieces of at most 1,000 characters. Shaded colours are chunks; amber text appears in two chunks (overlap). Hover to see which.

  4. 4

    Turn each chunk into a vector

    Every chunk becomes a list of numbers, one per vocabulary word, weighted so rare, distinctive words count most (TF-IDF here; a real embedding model in production).

  5. 5

    Store with metadata

    Each chunk is saved with an id, its text, its vector and its filename. The filename is what makes citations possible later.

Part B

Answering: one question, end to end

Try:
  1. 6

    Your question

    Exactly what a user would type.

  2. 7

    Split into tokens

    Lowercased and split into pieces. Very common words like how and the carry little meaning, so this representation drops them. Real LLM tokenizers go further and split rare words into sub-word pieces such as retriev + al.

  3. 8

    Look up token IDs

    Each word is replaced by its position in the vocabulary. Words the vocabulary has never seen can't be represented.

  4. 9

    Build the query vector

    One slot per vocabulary word, the same layout as the chunk vectors in Part A. Brighter cells are rarer, more distinctive words. Real embedding models produce a few hundred to a few thousand dense numbers instead.

  5. 10

    Place it in space

    Projected to 3D next to the 21 stored chunks. Drag to orbit, hover a point to read its chunk.

    3D needs WebGL, which this browser does not support. The position is listed below.

  6. 11

    Vector search

    Rank every stored chunk by cosine similarity to the query vector and keep the top 3.

  7. 12

    Keyword search (BM25)

    Independently, rank chunks by exact shared words. This catches names and codes that vectors can blur.

  8. 13

    Merge with Reciprocal Rank Fusion

    Combine both rankings into one list, as LangChain's EnsembleRetriever does in hybrid_search.py.

  9. 14

    Build the prompt

    The fused chunks are tagged with [Source: filename] and placed under the instructions from generation.py. This is the exact text that would be sent to Gemini.

  10. 15

    Grounding preview

    No model runs here, so there is no generated answer. These are the context sentences that share the most words with your question: the evidence a grounded answer would cite.

  11. 16

    Check the result

    Before trusting an answer, check the evidence: is it relevant enough, can every claim be cited, and for test questions, was the expected source retrieved?

Where this happens in the code

  • ingest.py: load, chunk, embed, store
  • hybrid_search.py: BM25 + vector + fusion
  • generation.py: prompt and answer
  • evaluation.py: the retrieval and generation checks
